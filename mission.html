Johns Hopkins Global Sight Initiative

Translational innovation to eliminate avoidable blindness and prevent visual impairment.

Our mission is to unite science, technology, and global partners — to find patients, restore sight, replace burdensome care, and scale the solutions that eliminate avoidable blindness.

01  /  The burden

Most blindness is not inevitable. It is a problem of scale.

The challenge is reaching people before disease becomes irreversible — in the places where specialists are fewest and the burden is heaviest.

2.2 billion
people live with a vision impairment worldwide.
WHO World Report on Vision
1 billion
of those cases could have been prevented, or remain unaddressed.
WHO World Report on Vision
What is driving it

Aging populations. Rising chronic eye disease. Widening gaps in access and workforce. And the cost of the infrastructure that eye care has always required.

02  /  The opportunity
Technology can extend expertise, simplify care, improve durability, and scale to the size of the need — turning specialist-dependent medicine into care that reaches everyone.
Transformative potential
03  /  Our approach

Four integrated pillars, one care continuum.

Each pillar attacks a different failure point — from finding the patient to scaling the cure.

01

Find patients & connect to care

AI-enabled screening, clinical decision support, referral tracking, disease monitoring, and precision prognosis.

02

Democratize sight-restoring procedures

Make cataract surgery, corneal restoration, infection treatment, and postoperative care simpler, safer, longer-lasting, and less dependent on infrastructure.

03

Replace burdensome chronic care

Deliver durable therapy for glaucoma, diabetic retinal disease, AMD, myopia, and other progressive conditions — reducing or eliminating repeated visits, injections, and drops.

04

Validate, translate, commercialize & scale

Coordinate clinical validation, regulatory and manufacturing planning, and engagement with foundations, industry, and investors to scale impact.

04  /  The portfolio

Addressing the leading causes of blindness.

Ten-plus technology programs span the full care continuum. Each pairs a defined global burden with a translational solution built to change the standard of care.

Program 01
Detection, triage & referral
2.2B with vision impairment
Today
Specialist-dependent exams cannot scale to volume, geography, or chronicity; many patients never reach a specialist.
Building
AI-enabled portable imaging, triage, referral tracking, decision support, and longitudinal monitoring.
The shift
Specialist-level decision support, extended beyond the clinic.
Program 02
Myopia & refractive disease
50% of the world by 2050
Today
Behavior change, atropine, or specialty lenses all require long-term adherence.
Building
Scalable topical myopia treatment paired with AI-enabled screening and monitoring.
The shift
Reaching a population-level epidemic before irreversible complications develop.
Program 03
Cataract
17M blind · 83.5M with MSVI
Today
Surgery requires capital equipment, trained specialists, and cost.
Building
Pharmacologic therapy to treat cataract, and a low-cost handheld surgical device.
The shift
Cataract treatment made simpler, cheaper, and more accessible.
Program 04
Corneal blindness
12.7M need a transplant
Today
Donor scarcity, complex surgery, rejection, and intensive topical dosing.
Building
Synthetic tissue and simplified, non-specialist surgery; sustained anti-infective depots.
The shift
Less dependence on donor tissue, specialists, and adherence-intensive therapy.
Program 05
Postoperative infection & inflammation
Tens of millions of surgeries
Today
Multi-drop regimens are poorly adhered to — and the patients least able to administer drops are most at risk of infection and inflammation.
Building
Drop-free, surgeon-administered, sustained antibiotic and steroid depots.
The shift
Postoperative pharmacotherapy turned from an adherence problem into controlled care.
Program 06
Glaucoma prognosis & treatment
80M today · 112M by 2040
Today
Slow detection; drops need lifelong adherence; surgery is short-lived; vision loss is irreversible.
Building
AI-enabled prognosis, topical neuroprotection, sustained IOP lowering, and programmable surgery.
The shift
From delayed detection to prognosis, durable IOP control, and neuroprotection.
Program 07
Diabetic retinal disease
103M adults, rising globally
Today
Anti-VEGF injections require 6–12 per year for years, with high cost and adherence burden.
Building
A one-time phototherapy implant targeting rod oxygen demand, retinal hypoxia, and VEGF signaling.
The shift
From repeated rescue injections to a long-duration implant targeting upstream biology.
Program 08
Age-related macular degeneration
196M today · 288M by 2040
Today
Dry AMD has limited options; wet AMD requires lifelong injections. It is a leading cause of central vision loss.
Building
AI-designed, sustained topical retinal delivery systems.
The shift
Repeated injections replaced with earlier, less invasive, more durable therapy.
05  /  From bench to bedside

The innovation and impact journey.

A disciplined path from unmet need to global impact — with data, outcomes, and feedback feeding back into every stage.

01

Need identification & prioritization

Global burden assessment, patient and partner input, and technical feasibility.

02

Discovery & invention

AI, nanomedicine, biomaterials, photonics, imaging, and proteomics.

03

Engineering & prototyping

Human-centered design, device engineering, formulation, benchtop and preclinical testing.

04

Clinical validation

Prospective studies, real-world evidence, and multisite, global care-system validation.

05

Translation & scale

Regulatory strategy, manufacturing, quality, supply chain, and cost optimization.

06

Implementation & access

Training, integration, reimbursement, policy, and sustainable delivery models.

07

Impact

Prevent blindness, restore sight, improve lives, and empower communities.

A continuous learning loop — data, outcomes, and feedback drive iteration and improvement.
